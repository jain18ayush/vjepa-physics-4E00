Part 1 of the V-JEPA physics take-home

Three moves that read motion out of a frozen encoder

Joseph et al. do not train a physics engine. They ask a frozen V-JEPA 2 encoder where speed, acceleration, and direction become linearly readable, how many dimensions each occupies, and whether those dimensions can be steered.

What Part 1 actually asks for

The supplied clips are a smaller, flatter cousin of the paper’s Kubric balls: one blue disk, 256×256, 16 frames, 24 fps. Direction has 1,500 clips and 64 angles. Speed and acceleration have 1,536 clips each, with 64 magnitudes. The goal is the same experimental sequence, and the same qualitative story, on this data. Matching the paper’s R² numbers is not the target.

MoveQuestionSignature in the paper
Layer-wise probesWhen is each variable linearly available?Speed and acceleration from the first layers. Direction jumps near layer 8 of 24, peaks in the middle, then softens.

Leave IntPhys, attention ablations, and the Goodfire splines out of this pass. Those are other sections of the paper, and Part 2 of the README.

Model, frozen

V-JEPA 2 ViT-L/16 at 256 resolution: facebook/vjepa2-vitl-fpc64-256. Twenty-four blocks, width 1024. The checkpoint name says 64 frames; the config notes that frame count does not change inference. Feed the 16 real frames. Do not tile them up to 64.

Tubelets of 2 frames turn 16 frames into 8 time steps. Patches of 16 on a 256 image give a 16×16 grid. Each clip is 8 × 256 = 2048 tokens.

Move 1. Probe every layer

Run the frozen encoder once per clip with output_hidden_states=True and skip_predictor=True. Keep the residual stream after each block. In Transformers, hidden_states[0] is the patch embedding and hidden_states[1] through hidden_states[24] are blocks 0 through 23. The paper’s layer index is the block index.

Mean-pool the 2048 tokens to one vector per layer. That is the probe the paper treats as the primary readout: a linear map of the pooled residual, f(h) = W h + b. Save the pooled tensor outside data/, about a quarter gigabyte in float16 for all three datasets.

Fit a separate probe at every layer.

  • Direction. Predict (sin θ, cos θ) with squared error. Score R² on that pair, then convert the prediction with atan2 and score circular mean absolute error. Chance on a uniform circle is about 90°.
  • Speed and acceleration. Predict the scalar magnitude with squared error. Score R² and MAE in the original units (m/s and m/s²).

The paper swept learning rate and weight decay and reported 5-fold grouped cross-validation. A ridge regression with a small alpha grid is the same linear model and is enough here, as long as the split is grouped. An independent reproduction found that pooling choice moves the curves a lot: mean-pool is what the paper states; last-time-step pooling was what made their Figure 2c line up. Start with mean-pool, write that choice down, and only then try last-time-step pooling if direction is already strong at layer 0.

What “emergence” means on a plot

Draw one chart with three curves, R² against layer. The finding to look for is an ordering, not a number: speed and acceleration high early, direction low and then rising near one third depth, all three a bit weaker at the last layer than at their peak.

Use the validation fold to name a working layer for the next two moves. In the paper that layer is 8. If this dataset shifts the rise, follow the rise. Do not hard-code 8 if the curve says otherwise.

Move 2. Remove the probe, then probe again

This is iterative nullspace projection, written out in Appendix C.11. It is the same idea as Ravfogel’s INLP, specialized to regression weights rather than a gender classifier.

X ← X − X Q Qᵀ   after each probe, where Q is an orthonormal basis for that probe’s rows.

  1. At the chosen layer, take training activations X.
  2. Fit probe k. Record validation R² and MAE.
  3. QR-decompose the probe weight matrix. For direction the weight is 2 × 1024, so each step removes two dimensions. For speed or acceleration it removes one.
  4. Project those directions out of X and refit.
  5. Stop when direction R² drops under 0.1 or circular MAE exceeds 80°, or when speed or acceleration R² drops under 0.05.

Effective dimension is 2K for direction and K for the scalars. The paper’s sawtooth on direction is the interesting shape: performance dips and partly recovers in pairs, which they read as sine/cosine partners. Speed should decay without that jag.

Move 3. Steer with many probes at once

Appendix C.12 turns the same probe sequence into an intervention. Stack the training-set probe weights and QR-decompose them into an orthonormal basis V. A single column of V is not enough. Steering means replacing the coordinates of x inside that whole subspace.

  1. Split activations into a fit set and a locked evaluation set before any probe is trained. The paper used 70 / 30.
  2. Build the orthogonal probe sequence on the fit set only.
  3. Train one evaluation probe on the locked set, using unsteered activations. It never sees the steering probes.
  4. For a chosen target angle θ*, project a locked activation into the subspace, solve for new coordinates so every included probe predicts (sin θ*, cos θ*), and add back the component orthogonal to V.
  5. Read the steered vector with the evaluation probe. Report circular error to the target and to the true label.

x⊥ = x − V Vᵀ x     x* = V c* + x⊥

c* is the least-squares solution of “every probe k should output the target sine and cosine,” including each probe’s bias and the leftover x⊥. Repeat for 1, 5, 10, and 20 probes. The causal claim is the opposing pair of errors: error to the target falls while error to the true angle rises.

3. Nullspace At that layer, iterate project-and-refit on train, score each step on val. Plot the curve. Count dimensions as 2K or K. Stopping thresholds are val metrics. Do not peek at test to decide when to stop.
4. Steer Build V from the train probe sequence. Train the evaluation probe on test activations only, before steering. Sweep the number of probes. Plot MAE to target and MAE to truth. The evaluation probe is not one of the steering probes.

Plumbing that is easy to get wrong

  • Normalize pixels with the V-JEPA processor. A hand-rolled 0–1 scale will not match the checkpoint.
  • Sixteen frames in, tubelet 2, so eight temporal tokens. If the processor resamples time, override it and pass the 16 frames in order.
  • Direction labels are degrees in metadata. Convert to radians before sine and cosine. Circular error wraps through 0° and 360°.
  • Speed and acceleration datasets also store a direction. Probe only the primary magnitude in those sets, or the direction factor will dominate a careless target.
  • Z-score scalar targets inside each fold, using training statistics, if you want R² comparable across layers. Still report MAE in m/s and m/s².
  • Expect the curves to be softer than the paper. Their velocity set had 8 directions and 392 Kubric renders. This set has 64 directions and a flat blue disk. The ordering of the three variables is the result that should survive.

shauli-ravfogel/nullspace_projection The reference algorithm behind “remove the linear readout and try again.” Read src/debias.py for the geometry. Their code removes a classification attribute from word vectors. The paper’s version is shorter: QR the regression weights and subtract the projector. Implementing that is about a page, not a port.
scikit-learn Ridge, GroupKFold, and r2_score. Enough for every probe in Part 1. No video model.

A finished Part 1

Three figures, each with the split and the pooling written on it. First, R² against layer for direction, speed, and acceleration, with a marked layer. Second, validation score against nullspace iteration at that layer, direction and one scalar on the same axes. Third, held-out circular error to the target and to the true angle, against the number of probes in the steering basis.

The interpretation the paper wants, and the one to test rather than assume: scalars are available early, direction arrives later and redundantly, and changing direction means moving many of those directions together. If the sawtooth or the multi-probe steering fails on this simpler video, that failure is a result. Say which choice (pooling, layer, target scaling) it depended on.

Sources: Joseph et al., Interpreting Physics in Video World Models, arXiv:2602.07050, especially Sections 5 and 7 and Appendices B, C.11, and C.12; the take-home README Part 1 and DATA.md. Curves and the steering dial illustrate the paper’s reported shapes. They are not measurements on these clips.
