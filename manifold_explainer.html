Steering is a path, and the path is a spline

Manifold steering replaces a straight line in activation space with a curve fit through the model’s own concept centroids. Walk the curve and the outputs pass through neighboring concepts. Cut the chord and the outputs jump.

How they find the spline

They do not discover the curve with an unsupervised manifold learner. The task already supplies an ordering — days, ages, grid coordinates, car position — and the spline is the smoothest curve through the average activation of each value on that ordering.

  1. Collect unintervened activations

    Run the frozen model on the task. For language, take the residual stream at one late layer (Llama 3.1 8B, layer 28, last token). For mountain car, take the encoder latent of each rendered frame.

  2. Drop to a subspace

    Language activations are projected with PCA to 64 dimensions. The spline lives entirely in that subspace. The leftover coordinates are kept untouched when you later steer. Mountain car is already 64-dimensional, so they skip this cut.

  3. Average one centroid per concept

    Every prompt with the same answer is averaged. Wednesday is one point, not a cloud. Mountain car has a continuous position, so they slice the range into 100 bins and average the encoder vectors in each occupied bin, weighted by the square root of the bin count.

  4. Choose the intrinsic coordinate

    This coordinate is the parameter the spline is drawn against. Sequential concepts use the ground-truth order (letter index, age). Cyclic concepts use the angle of each centroid in the top two principal components, θ = atan2(PC2, PC1), so the loop can close without being told the weekday names. Grids use the graph’s own (x, y). Mountain car uses position itself, scaled to [0, 1].

  5. Interpolate with a cubic spline

    One-dimensional concepts get a cubic spline through the centroids (Reinsch 1967). Sequential tasks use a natural cubic spline, second derivative zero at the ends. Cyclic tasks use a periodic cubic spline so Sunday meets Monday with matching slope. The fit passes through every centroid exactly. Two-dimensional grids use a thin-plate spline, the surface version of the same idea. Mountain car uses a smoothing spline, one univariate spline per latent coordinate.

The behavior manifold is the same construction on output distributions. Each distribution is mapped by a square root into Hellinger space, the spline is fit in the tangent plane of that sphere, and points are mapped back with the exponential map so every sample is still a valid distribution.

How steering uses the spline

A steering path is a curve from one activation to another. The model’s activation at the chosen site is replaced by a point on that curve, and the forward pass continues. Linear steering and manifold steering share the endpoints. They differ in the coordinate system of the interpolation.

linear: (1 − t) h₀ + t h₁

manifold: s( (1 − t) u₀ + t u₁ )

Here u is the intrinsic coordinate, and s is the spline that turns u back into an activation. On a circle, t moves the angle. On ages, t moves along the age index. The decoded activation stays on the curve the whole way.

At intervention time the spline point is lifted out of PCA with the inverse projection and added to the prompt’s original leftover coordinates. Only the top 64 components change. Linear steering, in their language experiments, replaces the whole residual.

They sample about 50 steps, average the resulting output distributions over a fixed handful of base prompts, and compare the trajectory to the behavior spline. Manifold paths shift probability onto neighboring concepts. Linear paths put mass on the endpoints at once — the teleport the demo draws in red.

The two checks

Along the activation spline. Does behavior follow the behavior spline? They also score cumulative Bhattacharyya distance from each output to the nearest point on that behavior curve. Lower means the outputs stayed on natural distributions.

Pullback, the reverse direction. Optimize an activation path so the outputs track the behavior spline. The optimized path lands back on the activation spline, even though it starts on the chord. That optimization is L-BFGS on a second spline’s control points. It is evidence, and it is not required to steer.

The isometry test is correlational and comes first: arc length between centroids on the activation spline versus arc length on the behavior spline. They report Pearson r near 0.99 on weekdays, letters, ages, and mountain car. Chord length in activation space correlates much less, and on mountain car it falls to about 0.06 because the loop folds.

Mountain car is the visual version

Section 5 is the piece that sits next to a video encoder. They train a small recurrent world model: a CNN encodes a 128×128 frame to 64 dimensions, a GRU mixes in the action, a decoder predicts the next frame as a residual. Position of the car is the concept.

Encoder states from 100 rollouts lie on a curve parameterized by position. Wall and goal look alike, so the curve nearly closes: two positions that are far apart in the valley can sit next to each other in activation space. A straight chord between them is short and meaningless. The spline follows the valley.

There is no vocabulary, so “behavior” is manufactured. Bin centers μ_b are samples of the activation spline. A latent z is turned into a distribution over bins by

p(z) = softmax( −‖z − μ_b‖² / τ ), τ = 0.5

That distribution gets the same Hellinger spline as the language outputs. Steering along the activation spline moves the decoded car smoothly up the hill. The chord decodes as a blur: a mixture of the start and end positions. The folded-loop mode in the demo is a cartoon of that fold.

What they measure on the car

A ridge probe on the encoder recovers position at R² ≈ 0.95 and velocity at R² ≈ 0.90, so the latent really carries the state.

Arc length on the activation spline versus arc length on the behavior spline: r = 0.996 over 50 anchor positions. Chord length versus behavior arc length: r = 0.06.

Pullback paths, optimized to match the behavior spline, sit 0.29 away from the activation curve, against 2.22 for the chord they were initialized on.

What this means for speed, direction, and acceleration

V-JEPA has no next-token distribution over a concept set. The mountain-car readout is the relevant substitute: a curve through activation centroids, and a probe or a distance-to-centroid distribution as behavior. Part 1 already located the layer and the linear baseline. Direction is the weekday case. Speed and acceleration magnitude are the age case, unless the cloud is thick enough that a single curve cannot carry them.
