Where a video model
stores motion

Direction, speed, and acceleration inside a frozen V-JEPA 2. First I check that a simple readout can see them. Then I ask what shape that storage has.

Space to advance. N for the cue on this slide.

The experiment

One blue disk, three questions.

Videos256×256, 16 frames. A blue disk on a dark field. Direction1,500 clips, 64 angles. Half coast, half speed up. Speed1,536 clips, from 0.25 to 4 m/s. Acceleration1,536 clips, from 0.25 to 10 m/s², starting from rest.
  • 1At which layer can a readout see each variable?
  • 2Is it stored once, or copied many times?
  • 3If I edit those copies, does the readout actually change?

Shared setup

I freeze the model and train a readout.

  • V-JEPA 2 ViT-L, 24 layers, never updated. The predictor is off. I feed the real 16 frames.
  • Each layer gives 2,048 tokens. I average them into one vector of 1,024 numbers. That vector is what every later experiment edits.
  • A probe is a straight-line readout from that vector to the label. For direction it predicts sine and cosine of the angle, then I turn that back into degrees.
  • Training fits the readout. Validation chooses the layer. Test clips are untouched until the final check. A random angle is wrong by 90° on average.

When it becomes readable

I probe every layer. Direction turns on at layer 5.

What I did

Train a fresh readout at each of the 24 layers. Choose the layer on validation only.

What I found

Speed and acceleration are already readable at layer 0 (R² 0.80 and 0.79). Steady-motion direction jumps from 0.23 to 0.93 at layer 5. Later slides use layer 5.

R² near 1 means the readout captures the label. The same jump in the paper was at layer 8. I do not see that fade by the last layer.

How many copies, at layer 5

I erase the readout and train another, until it fails.

82 Direction. 41 rounds before the angle is gone. The curve is jagged: sine and cosine take turns.
5 Speed. A smooth decline. It stops while a little signal is left, because error has reached the “just guess the average” line.
2 Acceleration. Smooth, and gone after two rounds.

Each round removes only the direction that readout was using, then a new readout is trained in what remains. Direction is written down many times. Speed and acceleration are not. The paper’s direction count near its jump was about 40–50, on 8 angles rather than 64.

Does editing those copies change the angle?

I try to make every test clip read as 90°.

On held-out clips, I edit the layer-5 vector only along the direction probes from the last experiment, so those probes output 90°. The rest of the vector stays as it was. A new readout, trained on unedited test clips, then says what angle it sees.

No edit

The new readout still knows the video: 19° off the real angle. It is 90° away from 90°, because the real angles are scattered all around the circle.

Edit 1 probe

I change the vector along a single probe. The reading moves toward 90° (58° away) and starts to leave the real angle (38°). One copy is not the whole store.

Edit 5 probes

I change those copies together. Now 11° from 90°, and 82° from the angle in the video. The readout has been moved onto the angle I asked for.

Against the paper

Same pattern, on an easier video.

  • Speed and acceleration are readable from the first layer. The paper found that too.
  • Direction appears in a jump, here at layer 5, in the paper at layer 8.
  • Direction takes many probes to erase, and the erasure curve is jagged. Speed takes a handful and falls smoothly.
  • Editing many probes on held-out clips moves the readout onto a chosen angle. I get there with fewer probes.

The next question: are those many probes many facts about the disk, or many copies of one circle?

Is direction a circle?

I walk a curve through the angles, and a straight line.

What I did

At layer 5, average the training vectors that share an angle, and connect those averages with a smooth loop. Also draw the straight line from 0° to 180°. Put each test clip on each path and read the angle.

What I found

Halfway, the loop is 5.5° off the angle it should be. The straight line is 171° off: the readout jumps across the circle. Both end at 18.6°. Editing 20 copies toward 180° ends tighter, at 8.5°, but it does not pass through the angles in between.

Same test for speed and acceleration

For speed, the straight line is enough.

What I did

Same two paths, from 0.5 to 3.5 m/s, and from 1 to 8 m/s². Read the magnitude at each step with a readout fit on unedited test clips.

What I found

Halfway along the speed path, the straight line is off by 0.10 m/s and the curve by 0.37. Curvature is not helping. Neither path reaches 3.5 under this readout; both end at 2.9.

Acceleration looks the same, and its readout is weak (R² 0.33), so the absolute errors there are soft. A short angle hop, 22° to 338°, also needs no curve: both paths finish 1.3° off, while 20 probes finish at 8.4°.

Is the curve the only path that works?

I bend the path until the readout is happy.

What I did

Start on the straight line from 0° to 180°. Nudge the path, in the top 32 components, until the frozen readout tracks the angle I asked for at every step.

1° Error of that bent path, at the middle and at the end. The readout got what it wanted.
3.3 How far the bent path sits from the curve. The straight line was closer, at 1.6.
12° Error of just walking the curve, in this run. Better behaved, worse score.

So the curve orders the angles, and plenty of nearby vectors still read as the right angle. That leftover room is the next measurement.

What are the extra copies for?

I walk the curve, then nudge sideways.

What I did

Pull each training vector onto the nearest point of the curve and remove the along-curve part. What remains sticks out sideways. Steering walks the curve and adds a little of that sideways offset so the readout hits the end angle.

What I found

Almost all the scatter is sideways (energy 26, against 0.03 along the curve). The curve alone ends 20° off. Two sideways directions end at 2.8°, and the middle of the path still follows the angles. Editing 20 copies with no curve, in this same run, ends at 3.5°.

What sits in that sideways scatter?

The curve knows the angle. The sides know the speed.

What I did

Predict each label twice: once from position along the curve, once from the first 10 sideways directions.

What I found

Along the curve, angle is predictable (R² 0.55) and speed is not. Sideways, speed is predictable (R² 0.70) and angle is not (R² −0.54).

Removing speed from the vectors shrinks that sideways energy by 28% and leaves most of it. A step along the curve moves the decoded angle by 8.8°. A step on the second sideways direction moves it by 0.14°.

The same measurement at four depths

Once direction exists, the sideways room grows.

What I did

Rebuild the curve and the sideways scatter at layers 0, 5, 12, and 23.

What I found

Sideways energy goes 3 → 26 → 50 → 221. A step along the curve moves the decoded angle by 29° at layer 0 and by under 1° at the output.

Walk the curve to pass through angles. Add about two sideways directions to land. Direction and speed share that sideways room.
